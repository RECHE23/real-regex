# v2026.10.2 — four wrong answers fixed, a tail after an inner literal kept on its route

**Four shapes answered wrong through the inner-literal route, in every release that has it, and this one fixes
them.** No error was raised: the search returned a later match than the leftmost, a shorter one, or the right
match with its groups split at the wrong place. Each answer was checked against Python's `re` and REAL's own
general VM:

| pattern | subject | before | now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `(xab\|a)bb` | `xabbb` | `[1,4)` | `[0,5)` |
| `(ba\|bb)+[ax]a` | `aabbbaaaaax` | `[3,7)` | `[2,8)` |
| `[abx]+ba[ab]+` | `aaaxbabaaxbab` | `[0,9)` | `[0,13)` |
| `(\w+)_(\w+)` | `a_b_c` | groups `a`, `b_c` | `a_b`, `c` |

**A pattern whose inner literal is followed by an optional tail keeps that literal's route**: `\w+://[^/ ]+/\S*`
was eight times slower than the same pattern without `/\S*`, and now runs at its speed. **A pattern with groups
fills them faster** where it is one-pass, and **the 10.1 slowdown of sparse alternation walks is gone**.

## Fixes

- **The inner-literal route answers the leftmost match and its groups** (0108613). The route confirms each
  occurrence of a required literal from the leftmost start its prefix reaches. A prefix that shrinks in fixed
  steps — an alternation of branches of unequal width, a loop over a body wider than one character — let an
  occurrence inside an earlier match's prefix reach only a later start, and confirm there; such a prefix now
  declines the route when it can hold every byte of the literal. The two-run confirm of `class+ literal class+`
  placed the literal at its first occurrence, where the greedy prefix leaves it at the last one: it is now used
  only when the literal cannot occur inside the prefix run, or when the suffix run crosses every byte it holds,
  and then splits the groups at the last occurrence. A differential fuzz of the route against the VM (every
  match and group of `find_iter`) diverged on 15 to 26 patterns in 20 000 before the fix; after it, none in
  more than 400 000, across seeds, alphabets, `(?i)` and UTF-8 subjects, every route compared.
- **A batched alternation walk no longer scans a spent subject twice** (8779091, c3dcb6c). 10.1 batched every
  alternation of four branches or more, and its walk refilled once more after reaching the end of the subject:
  `cat|dog|bird|fish` over 100 KB of prose (seven matches) went 9.1 → 12.1 µs (arm64), Python's `alternation
  findall` 11.6 → 13.0 µs. The rule now holds for every batched route: `\d+` over 1 MiB with one early match
  975 → 488 µs.

## Performance

- **A rare inner literal before an optional keeps its route** (cc1db2a): an optional anywhere declined the
  whole extraction. Over a 200 KB log, arm64: `\w+://[^/ ]+/\S*` 433 → 71 µs, `(\w+)@(\w+)\.(\w*)` 411 → 40,
  `\w+, ?\w+` 400 → 31, `\d+\.\d*` 321 → 62; x86-64 likewise (415 → 77, 385 → 45 µs). A dense literal (a space,
  an `e`) stays declined, and `https?://` keeps its prefix route. `static_regex` gains six to thirty-six times.
- **One-pass group extraction walks one flat table** (b8f55ab): `(\w+)@(\w+)\.(\w+)` over 62 KB of addresses,
  find_iter arm64 508 → 410 µs, x86-64 371 → 342; IPv4 and time groups −13 to −14 % (arm64).
- **A wide alternation verifies a candidate against its buckets' branches only** (01104d4), and **a walk over
  it keeps its scan between matches** (2126b09): twelve common words over issue #3's 500 KB corpus, arm64
  1 610 → 1 200 µs, x86-64 829.7 → 666.1 M instructions.
- A walk that reads no group no longer places them in the two-run confirm (0108613): `(\w+)_(\w+)` counted over
  identifiers 757 → 591 µs. Iterating it with its groups costs 762 → 835 µs, the price of groups that are right.

## Gates and tooling

- `fuzz-compat` no longer compares a lookahead under `match_not_null` on a standard library that applies the
  flag to the lookahead's own body (3979f5f): libstdc++ and libc++ find nothing for `(?=)a` over `"a"`, whose
  match is not empty. REAL's answer is pinned by a test instead.

measured on one host before and after; `CHANGELOG.md` lists them against the rows they touch. Python's `bench.py`
against §B, whose stamp predates 10.1 (median of three, arm64): `literal` 58.5 → 36.5 µs (10.1's pair filter), `alternation findall` 11.6 → 9.6, `emails findall

# v2026.8.14 — the instruments that were lying, and the target they found

**No row in `docs/BENCHMARKS.md` §A moves in this train, and that is a measured claim rather than an
omission.** What moved is per-call cost, one routing decision, and — more than anything else here — the
project's ability to tell a measurement from an artifact. Three separate instruments were reporting
silence as success; all three are fixed, and the last of them found the engine's largest single cost.

## Two engine changes, both now judged on both ISAs

**The second bulk copy per call is gone.** `basic_match_result` is constructed by the engine and filled in
place, so `run()` no longer builds a local slot storage and moves it in. The x86-64 leg was owed at
v2026.8.13 and is paid here, on an idle host with the governor pinned, 24 paired interleaved draws against
floors calibrated on that host:

| per-call row | x86-64 / gcc 15.2 | arm64 / Apple clang |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `short stamp match reject` | **−27.8 %** | −9.4 % |
| `short stamp match hit` | **−16.0 %** | −7.4 % |
| `short stamp search exact` | **−15.6 %** | −6.4 % |
| `short stamp search in` | **−14.1 %** | −8.4 % |

24 of 24 draws agree in sign on every row, and the other 22 rows are indistinguishable with medians inside
±1.0 %. **The x86-64 gain is two to three times the arm64 one, and the two legs differ by compiler as much
as by ISA:** gcc lowers that move to a `rep movsq` measured at 12.02 % of the inlined per-call path, and
`rep movs` pays a fixed startup whatever the volume — sixteen bytes here. Apple clang emits no such
instruction, so its leg only ever recovered the move itself.

**The Aho-Corasick gate stopped arbitrating on a quantity that cannot arbitrate.** v2026.8.13 published the
finding and deliberately did not act on it: the gate chose between the automaton and the cascade on
*candidate density*, which cannot tell a false start from a match, and those two pull in opposite
directions. It now also samples how many candidates complete a branch, and takes the automaton only when
fewer than 15 % do — the balance point on the conservative platform (15 % arm64 against 50 % x86-64), so
the gate can forfeit a win but never take one the cascade would have had.

| regime | arm64 | x86-64 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| candidates that complete | **−30.0 %** | **−18.4 %** |
| false starts only | +3.4 % | +0.12 % |
| prose, no candidate | −3.7 % | +0.00 % |

Verdicts move in one direction only — automaton to cascade, never the reverse — checked across ten
density/completion combinations. The cost is asymmetric by design: verifying a candidate costs one walk of
the split chain, which the cascade pays there anyway, and the expensive direction only happens in the
regime where the automaton then wins and amortises it.

**And the capture pool stopped allocating three times per general-VM call.** Chasing the one per-call row
`std::regex` still wins, the answer was twice not where it looked: `count_matches` alone is 96 % of that
row, and each branch of the pattern is fast alone — it is their union that falls to the general VM. The
fixed half was then counted rather than guessed: five heap allocations per call, 368 bytes, identical at
28, 256 and 1024 bytes, resolved by size *and by symbol*. Three of the five are the pool's `data`,
`refcount` and `free_list`, which had no small-buffer optimisation at all. **Five allocations become two.**
`pike.hpp` could not fix its own pool — it sits below `storage.hpp` in the layering contract, so its alias
has no `small_vec` within reach — but the pool is not a member of `basic_pike_state`, so choosing the
container in `storage.hpp` is the whole change.

## A refusal, with the table that refused it

Raising the thread lists' `mark` inline capacity from 8 to 16 would have closed the remaining two
allocations. It costs more than it buys, and the measurement says so: the four per-call rows move **+4.1 %
to +5.6 %** (19–22 of 24 draws agreeing) against −3.1 % on the row it was aimed at. No row is REAL by the
two-condition rule — which cuts both ways, and is why this is a refusal rather than a regression report.
With both sides under the bar, the tie goes to not growing a per-call object to save 176 bytes. The tier
that would cover the *measured* program-length band (11 to 73 instructions across eight realistic shapes)
is 64 or 128, i.e. +896 or +1920 bytes on a state already near 4944 — a worse version of the same trade.
Recorded beside the constant so the next reader inherits the measurement rather than the temptation.

## §A's prose contradicted §A's tables for three stamps

The tables were right. The reading bullets under them were carrying pre-v2026.8.11 ratios, and two of them
said the *opposite* of the cells printed directly above: `alternation` was filed as a PCRE2 win at 0.94×
where the table has read a REAL win since v2026.8.11 announced that crossing, and `lookahead` claimed
"within 3 % of PCRE2-JIT" at 0.97× where the table says 0.82× — a trade this project had already published
in its own release notes. Not one of eight range bounds appeared in the table. The count read "five rows
are REAL's on both ISAs" where the cells give seven.

**The checker that would have said so was wired to nothing** — not the Makefile, not any workflow — while
§A claimed in print that it "re-derives and checks all of them". Run for the first time it failed on two
cells, and a third once its rounding rule was made exact (a fixed relative tolerance cannot model displayed
precision: on a 0.21 ns/B cell, one unit in the last place is 4.8 % of the value). It now also reads the
PROSE — ranges, per-row pairs and the both-ISA count, 65 cell ratios and 18 bullet claims — refuses a
repeated row label, treats zero recognised claims as a failure, and runs from `make check-bench-ratios`,
local gate step 7b, `gate-doc`, and the **Docs-site** workflow, the one CI net with no `paths-ignore`.

**And §A's arm64 column was re-measured and CONFIRMED.** It had been declared uniformly too slow on the
grounds that its power state was unrecorded and an arm64 host had been caught throttling 29 % on battery elsewhere.
Three passes on AC reproduce every published cell within **−2.4 % to +3.6 % on REAL**, inside the inter-run
amplitude this document already declares. The published cells therefore stand: swapping in a second set
that differs by less than the declared noise would advertise a change that did not happen.

## A skipped step is no longer a passed step

`full-local-gate` prints a numbered list, and an optional leg that skips printed one warning line in the
middle of forty minutes of output. On the maintainer's own machine that meant **step 10 — `docs-site-gate`,
the net given no paths filter precisely so it always fires — never ran**, because `sphinx-build` is not on
that PATH. The run still ended on "ALL gates green". Step 20's `stubtest` had never run there at all.

Each optional leg now appends its own line to a ledger as it declines, and the end-of-run block only reads
that ledger, so it cannot drift from the branch that wrote it. The verdict line says "ALL gates green, NONE
skipped" only when the ledger is empty; otherwise it names each skip and how to close it. `GATE_STRICT=1`
refuses to pass at all — the form to use before a release. `make gate-venv` closes the two Python skips from
the same pins CI installs. Running `stubtest` for the first time on that machine reported no issues.

## Three instruments, and the target they found

`make bench-route-cliff` pairs route identity, per-byte cost and **match count** — the third because the
first version of that measurement read 91.6 ns/B for a pattern that matches nothing on its subject and a
recommendation was nearly built on it. `make bench-ac-gate` measures the gate's three regimes through
normal dispatch, which `ac_regime.cpp` cannot do because it forces the routes. And two profile counters
that had been declared for a long time and incremented nowhere are now wired, with their scope stated.

Those instruments then found what this train does not fix: **the general VM costs 12 to 25 ns per byte
stepped where a routed class loop costs about 1, and it takes 4911 of 7406 composed patterns.** A thread
histogram says why that matters — the trim shape carries ONE live thread on 100 % of its 2050 steps, so its
cost is bookkeeping with no parallelism to justify it, while `.*` shapes carry 8 to 15 threads on 61 % of
steps and are genuinely working. Nothing measured exceeds 15 live threads, and the programs behind them run
11 to 73 instructions: everything fits inside one 64-bit word. The pool traffic those steps generate is now
counted too — the trim shape performs 4833 refcount and copy-on-write operations to report a single match,
on a pattern with no capture groups.

That is the next train's work, and it is stated here as a target rather than a plan, because the change it
implies touches the hottest function in the engine.

## §B was not re-measured, and the stamp says so

Unlike the two trains before it, this one does not re-run the Python-binding comparison. `docs/BENCHMARKS.md`'s
Python row still stamps REAL `2026.8.13` — the version §B's figures were taken against — and it is left
there deliberately, so `make version-check` warns that the file is stamped one version behind. The warning
is accurate; bumping the row without re-running the benchmark is what would make it a false clean. §B's
dominant cost is the Python round trip rather than the scan, and this train's changes are per-call and
general-VM, so there is no reason to expect movement there. That is a prediction, and it is labelled as one
rather than published as a result.

## Nothing else changed

No answer changes anywhere in this train — every gate green, `fowler-compat` and `exhaustive-compat`
included, coverage 96.10 %. If you build the Python or Go bindings, the vendored headers move with the
engine as usual and nothing in their surface changes.

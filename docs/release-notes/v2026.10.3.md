# v2026.10.3 — groups found in one walk, a route that knows when to give way, a tenth of the headers gone

**A one-pass pattern with groups now finds its end and its groups in a single walk** where it read its window
twice, and **the inner-literal route bills what it reads and hands the subject to the core once it costs more**,
so a literal that is common inside long class runs no longer makes a search slower than the route-less engine.
**An optional before the inner literal keeps the route** again, now that the route can give way. The headers
lost a tenth of their lines — comments that narrated history, and code written twice — without a measurable
cost on any compiler this project is measured on.

## Fixes

- **A walk enters the inner-literal route once per subject** (a604ebed). A `find_iter` walk fills its batch
  before any search, and the batched filler read the route's abandon without the per-subject reset the search
  makes: an abandon there was keyed on the previous subject, the next search cleared it, and the route confirmed
  the subject a second time. `\w+ [\w ]+x\d{4}` over 200 KB of text without a newline: −20 % instructions on
  GCC 13 and GCC 15 (x86-64) and GCC 14 (aarch64).
- **Comments that contradicted the code are corrected** — about forty, among them `policy::strict`'s scope (a
  call REAL routes to `std::regex` runs on its backtracker under either policy), `uses_fallback()` (true for a
  non-`char` regex under strict too) and the `real/compat/std/regex.hpp` umbrella, which described a fallback
  that `policy::strict`, the default, does not take. `docs/COMPATIBILITY.md`'s contract now names the default.

## Performance

- **One-pass group extraction finds the end itself** (36417df): `(\w+)@(\w+)\.(\w+)` over 62 KB of addresses,
  find_iter arm64 409 → 294 µs; over a 200 KB log, URL groups 121.5 → 93.3 µs, addresses 63.5 → 54.0, times
  48.1 → 42.1, IPv4 53.8 → 49.1. A node whose edges sit on both sides of its match keeps the two-pass confirm.
- **The inner-literal route gives way when reaching its candidates costs more than the core's one pass**
  (a46b8c5): `[a-z]+ [a-z ]+x\d{4}` inside letter runs went from 1.20× slower than the route-less engine to
  1.00×; shapes the route wins on (`(\w+)@(\w+)\.(\w+)` at 0.08×, `\w+ \w+` at 0.42×) are unchanged.
- **An optional before the inner literal no longer declines it** (e332005): over a 200 KB log (arm64, the route
  against the engine without it), `\w+\s*:\s*\d+` counts in 0.13 of the time, `(\w+) ?= ?(\w+)` in 0.42,
  `(\w+)\s*=\s*"([^"]*)"` in 0.52; the shapes that lost through it (`[a-z ]*x\d{4}`, 1.61×) now give way at 1.00×.
- **`real::dfa::match(rest)` keeps two scalars rather than an optional per accepting byte** (20c9dbb): −6 to
  −14 % instructions on a seven-rule lexer, all three compilers; the memo-taking `match` is unchanged.
- **The trailing-lookaround class loop** (`[a-z]+(?=[a-z])`) −2 to −6 % (f329aba), and the `.`/negated-class
  route −3 to −5 % on UTF-8 text (c61aa4e).

## Refinement

The hand-written headers went from 40 742 to 36 648 lines. Every header had its comments reduced to the
constraints the code does not show; measured do-not-retry notes were kept, each in a line. Code written twice
became one: the test counters, the lane helpers shared by the NEON and SSE masks, the lazy DFAs' representability
test, the code-point width, the class-run scan of the trailing-lookaround loop, `real::dfa`'s three munch walks
and the lazy DFA's two walks with assertions. Each merge was accepted only where the instruction count stayed
equal or fell on GCC 13 and GCC 15 (x86-64) and GCC 14 (aarch64); five candidates that moved a row on one of
them were declined and are not in this release.

## Gates and tooling

- **A route differential fuzz** (`fuzz/route_differential.cpp`, `make fuzz-routes`, 9795134 and c3900c4)
  compares every `pike_vm` route with the general VM, with all route knobs off and with one knob alone; it runs
  in the local gate and in CI's fuzz job. Against v2026.10.1's headers its inner mode reports 24 diverging
  patterns in 20 000 — the four wrong answers v2026.10.2 fixed.
- `tools/sabotage.py` reverts its edit even when a signal lands during its own write (dd84924); a fourth
  self-test phase drives that instant deterministically.

## Sizes

`sizeof(real::regex)` 1 928 → 1 936 bytes (arm64, libc++), 1 944 → 1 952 (x86-64, libstdc++); the per-regex
immutables +8 bytes: the one-pass table's start node and its ends flag.

## Benchmarks

`docs/BENCHMARKS.md` keeps its `2026.9.11` + `79e6792` stamp: the tables were not re-run. Each change above was
measured on one host before and after; `CHANGELOG.md` lists them against the rows they touch. Python's `bench.py`
(arm64, median of three) against the published §B: every row within 5 % or faster — `literal · hit/miss @1MB`
58.5 → 37.3 µs, `emails · findall groups` 780 → 589 µs, `alternation · findall` 11.6 → 9.6 µs — but for the
known-unstable `sub · dates with refs`, whose medians spread 13.6–26.2 µs across the three runs and whose
minimum, 12.9 µs, is under the published 13.7.
